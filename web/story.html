01 / HOOK

Familiar factors do not identify risk

Three common assumptions about attendance, family size and paid classes do little to separate students by achievement.

Nursery attendance has only a small relationship with grades (about 0.4 points in mean final grade), family-size groups have medians one point apart and identical middle-50% ranges, and paid classes show no grade advantage (paid-class records average slightly lower). These weak results challenge the assumption that familiar demographic or financial factors automatically identify the students at most risk.

02 / RISING INSIGHTS

Goals, access and home context matter more

Three indicators show clearer separation, shifting attention from broad demographics toward student circumstances.

Students planning to pursue higher education have higher grades (mean 11.6 vs 8.4), students with home internet average about one point higher (11.6 vs 10.5), and students whose guardian is neither parent average about 1.3 points lower than those with a mother as guardian. These findings narrow the story from broad assumptions to indicators connected with student goals, access, and home context.

03 / MAIN MESSAGE

Past failure marks a concrete group for attention

Students with any recorded past failure are far more likely to fall below the passing threshold.

Among students with any past failure, 55.2% score below 10 and mean G3 is 7.99; among students with no past failure, 15.0% score below 10 and mean G3 is 12.05. Past academic history therefore provides the school board with a concrete group for early attention.

04 / SOLUTION

Start with prior-failure records

The recommendation uses existing records to target outreach, then tests whether the support actually helps.

Use prior-failure records to begin early outreach, assess whether internet access or guardian circumstances create additional needs, offer targeted academic support, and track later outcomes to evaluate whether the support works. The lower observed average among school-support recipients may reflect that support is assigned to students already struggling, not that support harms grades.
