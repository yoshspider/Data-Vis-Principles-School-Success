HOW DISADVANTAGES CONNECT

Which disadvantages travel together?

Each arc around the circle is a risk factor: context factors on the left, behavior factors on the right and past failure at the bottom. A ribbon joins two factors that occur together more often than chance, and its colour and width show how much more often (the lift).

  • Click an arc or ribbon to see its record count and pass rate.
  • The same records are selected in the matrix below.
  • Drag the lift slider to the right to keep only the strongest ties.
  • Esc, or a click on empty space, clears the selection.

Lift 2× = twice as often as chance (pairs with at least 10 shared records). Every factor gets an equal arc length; record counts and pass rates appear in a box when you click it. Arc thickness is the connection score: the sum of (lift − 1) over the ties shown. Ties between the same two groups are bundled together. An association, not a cause.

03 / MAIN MESSAGE

Past failure marks a concrete group for attention

Students with any recorded past failure are far more likely to fall below the passing threshold.

Among students with any past failure, 55.2% score below 10 and mean G3 is 7.99; among students with no past failure, 15.0% score below 10 and mean G3 is 12.05. Past academic history therefore provides the school board with a concrete group for early attention.

04 / SOLUTION

Start with prior-failure records

The recommendation uses existing records to target outreach, then tests whether the support actually helps.

Use prior-failure records to begin early outreach, assess whether internet access or guardian circumstances create additional needs, offer targeted academic support, and track later outcomes to evaluate whether the support works. The lower observed average among school-support recipients may reflect that support is assigned to students already struggling, not that support harms grades.
